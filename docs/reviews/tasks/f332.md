# F332 — charts, the drawing half

Done 2026-11-20. `lib/graphics/chart.zm`, tests. Designed first in
[charts](../../design/charts.md), which is **P1** of the port plan.

## The measurement made it small

Eleven files, one library. Counted across all of them: `LineChart` 35, `BarChart` 31,
`ComposedChart` 6, `ReferenceArea` 11 -- and **no pie, area, scatter, radar or brush at all**. So
two marks, not a grammar of graphics; building a general plotting grammar would have been building
the hardest thing for nobody, which is the same conclusion the catalogue measurement reached about
plural rules.

Two things that would have been got wrong by assuming:

- **The x axis is categorical**, not a time axis. Every one is `dataKey="date"`: evenly spaced
  points carrying date labels. A continuous time axis -- irregular spacing, tick selection, zoom --
  is most of the work in a charting library and none of it is needed.
- **The bands are horizontal**, `y1`/`y2`, drawn behind the data. This is the one feature here that
  is not decoration: a reading is meaningful against a range, and a chart that drew the line and
  left the range out has shown the number and hidden the meaning.

## It draws into a scene, not into a window

`lib/graphics` has a PDF backend and a raster backend, and this project exports thirteen formats.
**A chart that existed only as a widget could not go into an exported report** -- which is where a
chart is most often actually read. Drawing to a `scene.Scene` gets PDF and PNG for nothing, and the
widget half (F333) adds only the pointer and the accessibility.

## Two defects, both found by rendering and neither by a test

1. **A bar of the smallest value vanished.** Bars stood on the bottom of the range rather than on
   zero, so a reading of 1 against a range beginning at 1 drew nothing at all -- and an absent bar
   reads as absent data. **A bar axis now always includes zero**: a bar's length *is* the quantity,
   so an axis starting above zero draws a difference of one as a difference of everything, which is
   the oldest way there is to mislead with a chart. A *line* may be truncated and usually should
   be -- a weight between 80 and 83 kg plotted from zero is a flat line across the top of an empty
   chart -- so the rule is the bars' and not every series'.
2. **No y labels, in a gutter reserved for them.** The room was subtracted and nothing drawn in it,
   which reads as a margin rather than as a missing axis: the chart looked finished and asserted
   that its numbers were self-evident. The topmost label then needed a line of headroom, or it was
   drawn half outside the box and sliced.

Both tests were written against the old behaviour and had to be corrected, which is the honest
order: the render found the defect, the test was wrong, and the claim it now carries is stronger
than the one it had.

## Also decided here

- A series is `[float]` against `labels`, not pairs: the axis is categorical, so a pair would carry
  an x that is always its own index, and two series could disagree about where the third label is.
- A series longer or shorter than the labels is drawn **as far as the two agree**. Both happen
  while data is arriving and neither is worth refusing to draw the rest of the chart over.
- **A band is part of the y range.** A band outside the data's range would be clipped away, which
  redraws the chart's meaning in silence -- a reading sitting inside a normal range, drawn as
  though the range were somewhere else.
- A flat series is widened rather than divided by, since a range of zero height is a division by
  zero in every value's position.
