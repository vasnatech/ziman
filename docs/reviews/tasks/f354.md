# F354 — the same design, read again

Done 2026-11-27. `lib/ui/controls.zm`, two tests added.

[F353](f353.md) rebuilt the calendar against two pictures the health-record port's owner supplied
and the answer came back: **"datepicker does not look like the images I have shared."** It did
not, and this time the comparison was done the only way that settles it — the pictures and a
screenshot of the running window side by side, at the same scale, rather than a reading of the
pictures from memory.

Five differences, in the order the eye finds them.

## The chevrons were solid triangles

Every disclosure in this library wears `chevron`, a filled triangle: a combo box, a tree row, an
expander. The calendar wore it too, in four places — the month mark, the year mark and the two
paging arrows — and against a month's name in capitals a solid triangle reads as **a sort mark on
a column**, which is what `sort_mark` beside it actually is. The design draws all four as thin
lines.

`stroked_vee(p, a, q, dx, dy)` traces one as an outline and `vee_down`/`vee_up`/`vee_back`/
`vee_forward` place the four of them, each centred in its own box so that swapping one for
another does not shift it.

**Traced as an outline because there is no pen.** `widget.Drawn` carries a fill colour and nothing
else, so a stroke has to be a closed path: out along the vee and back along it offset by
`vee_band`. It mitres at the point by itself, because both arms meet it at the same angle — which
is also why the band's thickness is stated down the box rather than across the arm, and why what
anyone sees is `vee_band` over the square root of two, about a tenth of the box.

**`chevron` was not changed.** The filled triangle stays what the rest of the library wears. This
is a second shape for the one place a line is wanted, not a new look imposed on four controls
nobody asked about.

## The weekday names were a footnote, in the wrong place

Two things at once, which is why the row looked broken rather than merely small:

- They were set at `text.caption`'s size against a grid set at `text.body`'s, which makes seven
  column headings read as a footnote over the grid instead of the headings of it. They are at the
  day's size now, in the label's face — **the one thing in the design set heavier than the numbers
  under it, not lighter.**
- `head_in` gave them `calendar_cell * 0.7` and `widget.fixed_of` only *sizes*, so the names sat
  at the top of a band shorter than a row. The first week then floated a third of a row below
  them. A whole cell, and the names centred in it as a day is centred in its own.

## The grid was not square

`calendar_size` took its body from **the taller of the grid and the open list**, which F353 did
deliberately so that no list row could fall past the bottom edge. The cost was not visible until
the pictures were: six day rows sharing a band meant for twelve list rows made every cell a third
taller than it was wide, in the days view, **whether or not a list was showing.**

The popup is the grid's size now and the list fits itself into what that leaves —
`list_row_tall` divides the room among its rows and never gives a row more than it asked for.
In the plain look that is about half a point a row.

**And the clamp is load-bearing, which took two goes to establish.** Removing it and re-running
the suite left it green, so the test looked blind and the clamp looked like dead code. It was the
binary: `lib/**/*.zm` are bundled into it, so `./ziman` was still running the clamped library.
That trap is usually sprung by a window showing an old drawing; here it was sprung by a probe
printing old numbers, which is harder to disbelieve. Rebuilt, the same break overhangs the popup by 6 points in the plain look and 105 in
a look with a large caption, and takes three list rows out of reach with it.

## The header's four parts were butted together

The month mark, the year mark and the two arrows were laid edge to edge, which reads as one
four-part control rather than two questions and a pager. A step of `space.s` between the marks
and between the arrows.

**The gaps are subtracted from `head_boxes` rather than restated in `calendar_title`.** A constant
written once in the drawing and once in the press is `head_in`'s own twenty-two point offset
waiting to happen again; one box's edge taken from the next cannot drift.

## A chosen row was a pill, not a band

`list_panel` carried the side padding and the rows sat inside it, so the lit row was a pill
floating in the panel. The indent moved onto the rows and the heading, and the band now runs the
panel's full width — which is what a chosen row is everywhere else in this library, and in the
design.

## Watched fail

- **`every_row_of_an_open_list_is_inside_the_popup`** — every row of both lists, in two looks.
  Confirmed by returning `want` for a row the room cannot afford: **red**, and the probe beside it
  shows why — 55 points of overhang and row 11 answering as row 11's neighbour.
- **`a_day_cell_is_as_tall_as_it_is_wide`** — confirmed by giving the body 1.3 times the grid, as
  a list used to: **red**.

Neither reddens for the other's break, and the first one was green against the old binary before
`make` was run, which is the thing to remember from this one.

## What was not changed

The two departures from the pictures F353 recorded still stand, and both are still the
application's to overrule: **the week starts where the locale says** (Sunday here, Monday in the
pictures), and **`Select` commits** where a day press used to.

In the pictures the month list hangs past the bottom of the card and over everything below it.
Here it stops at the popup's own edge, because `widget.layer_at` only reaches what its parent's
bounds cover. Letting it out would mean the calendar handing the window **two** layers rather than
one — the popup and the list as siblings — which is a change to `calendar_layer`'s signature and
to every caller, and is the same gap [F345](f345.md) names from the other side.
