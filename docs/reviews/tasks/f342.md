# F342 — the button a form is expecting to be pressed

Done 2026-11-20. `lib/ui/controls.zm`, three tests.

A `Button` had `label`, `icon`, `badge`, `disabled` and `held`. It had **no way to be the important
one** — no primary, no suggested, no default. So a form could draw four identical buttons and say
nothing about which was the point of the form.

Every toolkit has this: GTK's `suggested-action`, macOS's default button, Material's filled against
outlined. Found by the health-record port's sign-in card, where the target fills "Sign in" with the
accent and leaves everything else outlined — and where the difference is visible on all four of the
signed-out screens.

## The mechanism was already there

`mood_of(disabled, pressed, selected, hovered)` takes four facts and `button_widget` was passing
`false` for the third. The whole change is a field and one argument:

```ziman
val parts = parts_for(style, mood_of(b.disabled, b.held, b.prominent, hovered_in(f, b.name)))
```

**Drawn with the style's `Selected` row**, which is the accent pair every look already defines
(`accent` on `on_accent`). A button is never "selected" in a list's sense, so there is no second
meaning to collide with, and no look needed a new row.

## The surprising half, which is right

`Selected` beats `Hovered` in `mood_of`'s precedence, so **a prominent button keeps its colour
under the pointer**. That rule exists for list rows — a selection that vanishes under the pointer
reads as the selection being lost — and inheriting it here looked at first like a side effect to
work around.

It is not. A prominent button is the one being offered, and repainting it in the neutral hover
colour would read as it turning into something else. The web application this was measured against
gives its primary button **no hover style either**: `disabled:opacity-60` and nothing more.

Stated as a test, because it is the part a reader will otherwise assume is a bug:
`a_prominent_button_does_not_change_under_the_pointer`, with
`a_plain_button_does_change_under_the_pointer` beside it so that the first is about prominence
rather than about hover not being drawn at all.

## What a look still cannot say

What a suggested button looks like **independently of a selection** — the two share one row of
`States`. No look has wanted to part them; the day one does, `States` grows a row rather than
`prominent` changing meaning.

And **at most one per group** is a thing a caller must mean rather than something this enforces:
two prominent buttons side by side say the same thing twice and so say nothing.

## Watched fail

Passing `false` again reddens two of the three: the accent test at "assertion failed" and the
under-the-pointer test at "expected != actual". The third stays green, which is correct — a plain
button's hover never depended on this.
