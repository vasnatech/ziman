# F353 — the calendar, against a design

Done 2026-11-26. `lib/ui/controls.zm`, three tests changed and two added.

The health-record port's owner handed over two pictures of a date picker and asked for the
library's calendar to match them, in this application's own colours rather than the pictures'.
Four things differed, and three of them were defects the pictures only made visible.

## The header said what it was, not what it did

`[◀] [October 2026] [▶]`, where the heading cycled Days to Months to Years when pressed. Three
things wrong with one control:

- **The year was reached through the month.** Two presses, and the first of them was about
  something else.
- **Which view a press gave you depended on which one you were already in**, so the same gesture
  meant three things.
- **The heading named the value, not the action**, which is why it had to carry the hidden label
  "Choose a month" for a reader while showing "October 2026" to everyone else.

Now `October ⌄  2026 ⌄` on the leading side with the two arrows together at the trailing edge.
Each mark opens its own list and closes it again, each says "Choose month" or "Hide month", and
each wears the chevron up while its own list is showing.

**`chevron_up` is a third shape and not `chevron(false)`** — that one points *right*, which is a
tree's closed row. A mark that turned from pointing down to pointing right would be saying "this
opens sideways", which nothing here does.

**And the header is hit-tested against the boxes it drew itself with.** It divided its width into
thirds before, which is a rule the drawing cannot follow and the press cannot check — `head_in`'s
own note records what that costs. `head_boxes` is asked once by each.

## The chosen day was a pill

The cell's own surface, rounded: a cell is wider than it is tall, so that is a pill the width of
the column, and seven of them in a row read as a bar rather than as one date. It is a **disc** now,
the shorter of the cell's two sides, in the accent with `on_accent` for the figure — `Radius.
Fraction(0.5)` rather than a number, so it is a disc at any type scale.

## The weekday headings read as data

"Sun Mon Tue" in the disabled ink, above a grid of two figures: three letters is a word, and a
faded word over a column of numbers reads as a quieter row of the same thing. Two letters in the
window's own ink, which is what every calendar draws.

**Cut here rather than asked of `time`.** `EE` is not a width every locale's formatter answers the
same way — some give two letters, some repeat what `EEE` gives, and one gives a single initial
that makes Tuesday and Thursday the same column.

## A press on a day was a decision nobody confirmed

The calendar wrote the date and shut. That is right for a menu and wrong for something a user
walks around in: paging to a month, trying a day, looking at the week around it, changing their
mind. There was nothing to confirm and nothing to take back.

`Cancel` and `Select` under the grid. A press on a day moves the highlight; `Select` writes it and
`Cancel` puts back both the value **and** the month the user walked to, or re-opening would show
the month they backed out of.

**`at` was already documented as "not the value"** — "a user paging through months with the arrows
has chosen nothing yet, and a calendar that moved the value as the highlight moved would write a
date into the field on the way past". The press has caught up with the comment.

**What it costs**: two gestures where there was one, for every date in every form. That is the
design's bargain rather than this library's opinion, and it is the one change here that an
application might reasonably want back.

## Watched fail

Three existing tests had to change, and each change is a statement:

- `the_title_zooms_out_through_months_to_years` — the marks are independent, so choosing a year
  puts the **days** back rather than handing on to the months.
- `the_grid_starts_on_the_locales_own_first_day` — asserts on where the press lands (`at`), not on
  what it writes, because a press no longer writes.
- `the_calendars_header_names_its_three_buttons` — `/zoom` is `/month` and `/year`, and what each
  says changes with whether its own list is up.

`a_day_is_chosen_by_select_and_taken_back_by_cancel` is the new one, and it asserts the three
things that make the footer worth having: the press does not change the field, `Cancel` restores
the walk as well as the value, and `Select` writes what is highlighted.
