# F350 — an overlay scroll bar, over a border

Done 2026-11-26. `lib/ui/widget.zm` (`Viewport.gutter`, `scroll_with_gutter`, the `Scroll` case of
`place`), `lib/ui/controls.zm` (`bar_gutter`, `viewport_of`), four tests.

Raised by the health-record port: "In Emergency Screen right border is not visible. It is behind
scrollbar."

## What happens

The emergency summary is a bordered card stretched to its viewport. Its **right-hand border is not
drawn** — or rather it is drawn and then painted over by the scroll bar, which is opaque. Scanned
across one row of the running window: the left border is four pixels of `#a04040` at x 416–419,
and on the right the card's fill runs to 1941 and the bar's thumb begins at 1942. No border at all.

## Why

The bar is an overlay, deliberately:

> **Over the content, not beside it**, which is what overlay bars do everywhere and what keeps this
> from being a layout change … What it costs is the last few points of a row, which on a list of
> text is the empty end of a line.

Two things are wrong with that bargain rather than one.

**The cost is not always an empty end of a line.** It is whatever the content puts at its trailing
edge, and a card puts its border there. The comment picks the case where the cost is invisible and
reasons from it.

**And an overlay bar is honest only while it is transient.** That is what makes it bearable on a
platform that fades one in on a gesture and out again: it covers the content for as long as you are
looking at the bar instead. This library's bar is drawn whenever the content overflows and never
fades, so it is a classic bar laid out like an overlay one. The paragraph above it even says "why
it is translucent" — the track is painted `Paint.Solid`.

## The fix

`Viewport.gutter`: how much of the trailing edge to keep clear for a bar, in points.
`widget.scroll_with_gutter` makes one; `named_scroll` and `scroll_of` make one with no gutter, so
every caller written before this is unchanged.

The `Scroll` case of `place` already measures its child to decide how big the content box is. It
now narrows that box by the gutter — **and only while the content overflows**, so a viewport whose
content fits uses its whole width and a window with nothing to scroll looks exactly as it did.

**One pass, and it cannot oscillate.** The obvious objection to reserving space only when there is
a bar is the browser's circular dependency: narrowing the content can make it taller, which can
make a bar appear, which narrows it again. The implication only runs one way — narrowing content
never makes it *shorter* — so content that overflowed at the full width still overflows at the
narrowed one, and the first answer is the final one. No second layout pass, which matters on a
window whose layout phase is already the expensive one.

**`controls` owns the number.** `bar_gutter(style)` is `style.space.m`, and it is now what
`bar_ops` draws at, what `on_the_bar` hit-tests against and what `bar_box` reserves — the three
numbers that used to be three copies of one expression. `controls.viewport_of(name, dx, dy, child,
f)` is the call an application makes instead of `widget.named_scroll`: `widget` can neither draw
the bar nor ask a look how wide it is, and a number written out at each screen is a number one
screen will be missing.

The toolkit's own four scrolled controls — `List`, `Tree`, `Table`'s body and `Entry`'s text
area — pass it too, so a selected row's trailing edge is no longer under the bar either.

**Named `viewport_of` and not `scrolled`**: `scrolled` is already a private step function in
`controls`, and ziman reads one name as one overload set — a public member beside a private one is
a set nothing outside can call, and the error it gives says the *public* function is private.

## Watched fail

| break | what reddened |
|---|---|
| the room is never kept | `a_viewport_that_scrolls_keeps_its_gutter_clear`, `a_viewport_stops_its_content_where_the_bar_starts` |
| the room is kept whether or not a bar is drawn | `a_viewport_that_fits_uses_its_whole_width` |
| `bar_gutter` answers something other than what the bar is drawn at | `a_viewport_gets_a_track_and_a_thumb` |

The third break leaves `a_viewport_stops_its_content_where_the_bar_starts` green, and that is
right rather than a gap: it asserts the *relationship* between the room and the bar, and the break
moved both together. The existing test is what pins the value. Between them one cannot drift from
the other without something going red.
