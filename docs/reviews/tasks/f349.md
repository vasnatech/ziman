# F349 — one look, one row air, and a window full of different lists

Done 2026-11-26. `lib/ui/controls.zm` (`List.air`, `list_row_height`), three tests.

Raised by the health-record port as "combobox item heights are taller than needed".

## What happens

A combo box drops a list of rows **40.1 points tall out of a 28.1-point box** — measured in the
running window, at the application's own look. Nine items need 361 points where the control they
came from is 28, and a dropdown reads as a menu of buttons rather than as a list of values.

Nothing asked for it. The application the port copies uses a native `<select>`, so its dropdown
rows are the operating system's.

## Why

[F343](f343.md) gave a look `Style.row_air` — "how much air a list's row has above and below its
line of text, which is what makes a list comfortable or dense" — and `row_height` is
`line_for(body) + row_air` for every `List`, `Tree` and `Table` in the window.

The port set `row_air: 24`, because that is what its **navigation rail** needs: the target's
`py-2.5` is ten points above and ten below a `text-sm` line, forty in all, and ziman measures that
line at sixteen rather than twenty, so the air carries the difference.

A combo box's popup is a `List` too. So is a menu's, and an autocomplete's. One number was being
asked to describe a rail somebody scans down and a dropdown somebody points at once.

**F343's own comment names both cases and gives them one answer**: "`space.s`, four, which makes a
row a line with a hairline of air — right for a dense table and wrong for a menu somebody points
at". The sentence is correct about there being two kinds and stops one step short.

## The fix

`List.air: Option<float>` — `None` takes the look's `row_air`, which is what every list did and
most should. `controls.list_row_height(l, m, style)` is what a list's own height is now asked
through, and `list_widget`, `list_input`, `list_at_end`, `autocomplete_popup_height` and
`context_menu_box` all ask it.

**This is not `row_height` going back onto the control.** F343's argument against that still
holds — `List`, `Tree` and `Table` each carried `row_height: 20.0`, "a line of twelve-point text
and a little air, written down once as a number and thereafter true only by coincidence". What a
list may now say is the **air**, which is still added to a line that is still measured: a look that
enlarges its type still gets rows that fit, whether or not the list asked for its own air.

**The look still says the default.** A window where every list is the same is unchanged and says
nothing; the one list that differs says so. That is the right way round for the port, where the
rail is the exception and every popup is the rule.

**`Tree` and `Table` do not have one.** Nothing has asked, and the honest reason to add a field is
that something did. They take the look's answer, as before.

## Watched fail

| break | what reddened |
|---|---|
| `list_row_height` ignores `l.air` | `a_list_can_ask_for_its_own_row_air` |
| `list_widget` reads the look's number while `list_input` reads the list's | `a_lists_own_air_moves_its_rows_and_its_presses_together` |
| a list's air *replaces* the measured line instead of surrounding it | `a_list_can_ask_for_its_own_row_air`, `a_lists_own_air_still_sits_round_a_measured_line` |

The first break leaves the press test green, and that is correct rather than a gap: with the air
ignored everywhere, the widget and the hit test still agree, which is the only thing that test
claims to check.

## What is still not sayable

**A row's air is one number, not a padding** — F343's note, unchanged.

**A popup cannot be denser than the window it opens over without being told to be.** The library
has a word for "in front of the window" already (`Style.raised` is documented as being for exactly
three things: a menu's popup, a combo box's list, a dialog's panel), so a look *could* say "lists
in front of the window are this roomy" and spare an application from saying it per list. Nothing
has asked, and a per-list answer is the more general one, so this is written down rather than
built.
