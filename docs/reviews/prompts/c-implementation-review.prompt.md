## What to evaluate
Cite `file:line` for everything, and prefer a demonstration to an assertion: a compiler invocation, a sanitizer trace, a crashing input, a disassembly, a measurement.

1. **Undefined and unspecified behaviour.** Hunt it deliberately. Build with sanitizers and report what they find — there is **no sanitizer target in the Makefile today**, so add your own invocation out-of-tree (ASan, UBSan, and TSan against a threaded fixture; note that Boehm needs care under ASan and say what you did about it). Look for: strict-aliasing violations and type punning (`value.c` unions, the `__atomic` compare-exchange on a `double`'s bits in `native_http.c`/`native_metrics.c`), unsequenced side effects and argument-evaluation-order assumptions in macros (`src/zgc.h`'s redefinitions are macro-expanded over expressions that read memory the call may move — one bug of exactly this shape is recorded in DESIGN.md, so check whether others remain), signed overflow (the build uses `-fwrapv`; find where the code relies on it and whether that reliance is stated), shifts, alignment, `memcpy` with overlapping ranges, flexible-array and trailing-array patterns in `mapobj.c`'s nodes, integer conversions between `int`, `int64_t`, `size_t` and `long long`, and anything that only works at `-O2`.
2. **Memory.** The two-collector arrangement is the riskiest thing in this codebase, and the one most worth your attention. Under the collector build `free` is a no-op and under `nogc` it is real, so a pointer's lifetime differs between builds — DESIGN.md records a use-after-free that was invisible in one build and segfaulted every other run in the other. Look for more of that class. Check: what is a GC root and what is merely reachable by luck (the conservative stack scan is load-bearing — `handles.c` deliberately keeps its live-handle table unscanned), `GC_MALLOC` vs `GC_MALLOC_ATOMIC` choices (is anything holding pointers allocated as atomic?), `GC_FREE` uses, `mmap`/`munmap` in `arena.c`, interior pointers given `ALL_INTERIOR_POINTERS`, and allocation inside a collection's mark phase. Run the `gc_*` unit tests and the `nogc` fixture suite repeatedly (intermittent failures are the signature here).
3. **Threads.** 17 files touch `pthread`. Audit every shared mutable object for a race: the `__atomic` builtins' memory orders (is `__ATOMIC_RELAXED` right where it is used, and is anything relying on an acquire/release pair that is not there?), the fixed tables guarded by one mutex in `native_http.c` and `native_metrics.c`, the per-thread pools and `__thread` state in `interpreter.c`, signal handling and what is actually async-signal-safe, and thread start-up/teardown with Boehm's thread registration. TSan on the concurrency fixtures is the evidence to bring.
4. **Robustness at the edges.** Fixed-capacity tables are declared with a `#define` and a comment (`MAX_ERRORS 25`, `MAX_RECOVERY_DEPTH 512`, `RATE_SLOTS 4096`, `RATE_KEY_MAX 96`, `GC_MAX_STRUCT_CONTENT_MARKERS 8`, `MAX_DEPTH 12`, `MAX_STACK_MB 768`, and others): for each, what happens at the limit, is it checked, and is the behaviour at the limit the documented one? Check allocation-failure paths (`realloc` returning NULL is often unhandled — say whether that is a defensible choice for this program or a latent crash), `snprintf` truncation, string handling where a Ziman `string` is **not NUL-terminated** (`value.h`'s `str` has `ptr`/`len`/`term`; `value_cstr` exists for a reason — find places that use `.ptr` directly and are wrong), recursion depth in the parser/typechecker/interpreter against the guard in `stack.c`, and the lexer/parser on hostile input (fuzz them: feed random and mutated `.zm` files and report every crash or hang with a minimised input).
5. **Portability and the build.** The project is Linux-only today and says so. Separate "would not compile elsewhere" from "would compile and be wrong": POSIX and glibc assumptions (`__GLIBC__` guards, `mallopt`, `/proc`, `pthread` specifics, `mmap` flags), endianness and word-size assumptions, C11 features used and whether the `-std=c11` promise holds. Try at least one other compiler (clang) and a higher warning level than the Makefile's `-Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic` (`-Wconversion`, `-Wshadow`, `-Wstrict-prototypes`, `-Wwrite-strings`, `-Wcast-qual`, `-Wmissing-prototypes`, `-fanalyzer` if available) and report what they say — triage the noise, but name the real findings. Comment on the Makefile itself: the wildcard source list, the force-included header, the vendored collector's flags, dependency tracking, and whether the gates actually gate.
6. **Maintainability as C.** Judge the architecture a new maintainer meets: module boundaries and header hygiene (what is `static` that should be, what is exposed that should not be), the size and branching of the largest functions, duplicated logic, ownership conventions and whether they are stated where they matter, comment quality (this codebase comments heavily — say where that genuinely helps and where it substitutes for simpler code), the value representation's cost and clarity, and the error-handling discipline (`ziman_error` and friends: what unwinds, what leaks, what is left half-initialised). Say plainly what you would refuse to maintain and why.
